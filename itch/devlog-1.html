Post title: Twelve days in a war diary: the raid on New York that the navy was asked to pay for. Type: Game Design (or Update). Image: itch\screenshot-4-file.png (the entry of 17 July) or screenshot-2-request.png. Open this file in the browser and copy from the first paragraph down.

The story of a German air raid on New York usually comes in one of two forms: the "Amerikabomber", four-engined landplanes that never existed in numbers, or a legend about a Ju 390 that flew to within sight of Long Island in 1944. Neither is the most solid trace. That one is in the war diary of the German Naval Staff, which the US Navy's Office of Naval Intelligence translated in full after the war, and which anyone can read today in scanned form.

On 16 July 1942 the diary records, "in a very restricted circle", that Göring is preparing a raid on New York by two Blohm & Voss BV 222 flying boats, "at the Fuehrer's request", and wants the navy's support. A surface ship as a tanker is out of the question; the large type IXc U-boats might do. Dönitz thinks it technically feasible. Then the Naval Staff states its price in the only currency it used: two U-boat missions, "during which 60,000 to 80,000 GRT of enemy shipping could be sunk".

The next day the procedure is written down. The flying boats will wait in France for the go-ahead from Base 1, "1000 km. off the enemy coast", land beside the U-boat, take fuel through two hoses for one and a half to two hours, take off at dusk, and come home via Base 2 north of the Azores. Not before 10 September; one plane or two still undecided. On 28 July: "The Air Force General Staff declined to participate in operation "BV 222" (raid on New York)." No reason is given, and the diary never mentions it again.

One phrase, two readings. "1000 km. off the enemy coast" sounds precise, but it is not. Measured from New York, Base 1 lies within reach of the patrol aircraft based in Nova Scotia and Bermuda. Measured from any coast, it is safe from them, but then the flying boat has some 5,400 km to cover from Base 1 to Base 2, close to the limit of its range. In the study this ambiguity becomes a decision, and the model shows the price of each reading: the cautious one loses fewer U-boats and more aircrews.

What the diary does not say. It names no purpose. The Nazi leadership's own documents of the same months do say what New York meant to them: in December 1941 Rosenberg noted that his speech's "comments about the New York Jews" might need changing, and Hitler said the Jews "had burdened the war upon us". The study sets these papers beside the war diary in a chapter of their own, and keeps them apart from the order: no document says the raid was meant for that reason, and a study that claimed so would be inventing evidence. A separate chapter turns to the New York the file does not count, the city of seven and a half million under the dim-out of 1942, photographed by Marjory Collins in the month the raid was due.
